Motivation & Academic Scope Slide 2 / 12

Problem Motivation & Why Nedumangad?

The Homogeneous Mixing Fallacy

  • Classical ODE models ($dS/dt = -\beta SI/N$) assume everyone in a town meets with equal probability.
  • They cannot represent road choke points, population density gradients, or high-contact facility mixing.
  • Cannot inform spatial decision-making like road barricades or targeted hub closures.

Why Nedumangad Municipality, Kerala?

  • Geographical Node: Primary transit junction connecting highland agro-ecological zones (Ponmudi, Vithura) with the capital city (Thiruvananthapuram).
  • Semi-Urban Landscape: High-density commercial core surrounded by semi-rural wards.
  • Kerala's Local Self-Governance: Decentralized municipal administration requires spatial decision support.
Speaker Note: "Classical mathematical models can estimate aggregate numbers, but they cannot tell municipal officers which road or market is accelerating the spread. We bridge this spatial gap."

Methodology • Diurnal ABM Engine Slide 6 / 12

Agent Mobility & Transmission Formulation

Diurnal Activity Routine

  • 00:00 - 07:00 (Night): Agents rest at residential home-patch.
  • 08:00 - 09:00 (Morning Commute): Transit along road network.
  • 10:00 - 16:00 (Daytime Hub Mixing): Dwell at assigned activity hubs. Intense contact occurs.
  • 17:00 - 18:00 (Evening Commute): Return transit along road lines.
  • 19:00 - 23:00 (Neighborhood): Local residential walks.

Dual Transmission Mechanics

1. Indoor Hub Superspreading:

P_hub = 1 - (1 - β_hub × Risk_h × μ_crowd / 2)^I_h × (1 - η_mask)

2. Street Encounters:

P_street = β_street × (1 - η_mask,sus) × (1 - η_mask,inf)
Speaker Note: "Agents follow daily rhythms. Transmission occurs in crowded indoor facilities governed by non-linear saturation, as well as outdoor street encounters."
Experimental Policy Sensitivity Slide 7 / 12

4 Comparative Policy Scenarios (Sample Benchmark)

Scenario A: Baseline (Unmitigated)

No travel bans, no closures, no active quarantine. Serves as the experimental control benchmark for natural spread in our sample.

Scenario B: Targeted Hub Closures (Day 8)

Shuttering high-density commercial markets (Valiya Chanda, Pazhakutty) and KSRTC bus stand. Domestic local travel permitted.

Scenario C: Blanket Municipal Lockdown (Day 10)

75% movement restriction across all wards. Non-essential vehicular and pedestrian movement curtailed.

Scenario D: Active Testing & Quarantine (Day 7)

35%-45% active infectious case detection with strict home/hospital isolation. General road mobility stays open.

Speaker Note: "We test four stylized policy interventions. Rather than focusing on exact case numbers, we analyze relative policy performance and curve shapes."

Results • Spatial Patterns Slide 9 / 12

Spatial Gradients: Urban Core vs. Rural Periphery

Central Commercial Core (Elevated Relative Spread)

  • Wards centered on the market and transit nucleus (Valiya Chanda, Town Ward, Melamcode, Pazhakutty).
  • Exhibit higher relative attack rates in the sample ($\approx 18\% - 22\%$).
  • Driven by settlement density and high commuter footfall from outer catchments.

Peripheral & Rural Wards (Naturally Insulated)

  • Peripheral wards along the northern and eastern municipal limits (Kurupuzha, Vettinadu, Panavoor Border, Palode Road).
  • Sustain significantly lower sample attack rates ($\approx 4\% - 7\%$).
  • Protected by spatial buffer, low building density, and dispersed agricultural settlements.

Methodological Takeaway: The model demonstrates how spatial settlement patterns create natural disease gradients, providing computational evidence that localized micro-containment cordoning is more rational than municipal-wide shutdowns.

Speaker Note: "The spatial pattern shows an illustrative gradient: central commercial wards sustain elevated transmission, while outer agricultural wards remain naturally buffered, supporting targeted micro-containment."

Strategic Observations Slide 11 / 12

Top 5 Methodological & Strategic Observations

  • 1. Spatial Structure Shapes Transmission: Disease transmission funnels along major structural road corridors rather than diffusing uniformly.
  • 2. Activity Hubs Amplify Spread: High-contact facilities (markets, bus stands) act as multipliers; shuttering them provides substantial mitigation without general curfews.
  • 3. Active Case Removal Outperforms General Lockdowns: Testing and isolating active cases reduces cumulative infections far more effectively than blanket curfews.
  • 4. Spatial Heterogeneity Supports Micro-Containment: Distinct transmission gradients between commercial centers and rural peripheries justify ward-level cordoning over town-wide closures.
  • 5. Spatial Inequity Requires Decentralized Triage: Peripheral wards face longer travel distances to the town hospital, highlighting the need for mobile screening units during outbreaks.
Speaker Note: "These five takeaways highlight the conceptual and practical insights gained from our spatial modeling approach."
Limitations & Conclusion Slide 12 / 12

Study Limitations & Conclusion

Academic Limitations

  • Sample-based scaling ($N = 1,500 - 3,000$) rather than 1:1 census simulation.
  • Official 2024 Delimitation Commission (K-SMART) cadastral ward boundaries integrated.
  • Closed municipal boundary envelope (excludes external inter-district highway transit on SH-2).
  • Parameterized indoor risk rather than 3D CFD ventilation physics.

Future Research Roadmap

  • Integration of anonymized cellular GPS/CDR mobility matrices.
  • Official cadastral property tax GIS layers from Kerala LSGD.
  • Wastewater-based epidemiology (WBE) drainage modeling.
  • Vaccine prioritization modeling for frontline transport workers.

Conclusion: This project establishes a reproducible, open-source GIS-ABM framework for Nedumangad Municipality, proving that coupling spatial network topology with agent-based simulations provides actionable insights for spatial epidemic management.
